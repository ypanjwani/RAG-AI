How to use this explainer

No coding or AI background needed. Pick whichever way of learning suits you.

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is how an AI answers questions about your document: it first finds the parts of the document that matter, then hands only those parts to the AI to write an answer. This page lets you watch every step of that happen for real, right in your browser.

Before you start: pick a document

In the left panel, keep the built-in sample article (a good choice for your first visit) or upload a PDF of your own. Only the first page of a PDF is used. Click View to read the full text.

Then choose one of the two ways below. You can switch between them at any time.

💬 Way 1: Learn by chatting

Quickest. Ask first, then look under the hood.

  1. In the right panel (“Ask your document”), type a question about your document, or click a suggested one, and press Send.
  2. Watch the row of numbered chips (1–9). Each one lights up as that step of the pipeline runs. Hover over a chip to see the step’s name.
  3. Read the answer. The Sources show which pieces of the document the AI was given.
  4. Click any ✓ chip to open that step in the middle of the page and see exactly what happened for your question.
  5. Below the answer, click 🧩 Chunks to see every piece of the document ranked by how well it matched, or ⚙️ Change parameters to tweak the settings and Regenerate, then compare the old and new answers.

🗂️ Way 2: Walk through the cards

Slower but deeper. Build the pipeline yourself, one step at a time.

  1. On the main page, the cards show the 9 steps in two groups: Indexing (preparing the document, done once) and Querying (what happens each time someone asks).
  2. Click Step 1 to begin. Each step explains the idea in plain words and gives you something to try.
  3. Finish what the step asks (for example, click a button or type a question), then press the next-step button at the bottom of the page.
  4. Steps unlock in order, so a 🔒 card just means “finish the earlier steps first”.
  5. Play with the sliders and options (chunk size, search method, how many chunks to keep) to see how each choice changes the final answer in Step 9.

Tips

  • The first time, the page downloads a small AI model (about 23 MB). After that it loads instantly.
  • Click ← All steps at the top of any step to go back to the cards.
  • The side panels can be hidden with the panel icon if you want more room.
